# Project questionnaire

Use this as an internal question bank, not a form shown to the user. Ask at most three questions per round, preferably one or two. First extract facts from the company name, public information, and uploaded materials. Choose only broad questions based on what remains unknown.

## Essential profile

Obtain enough information to determine:

- **Purpose/project type**: Why is diligence being performed? Examples include IPO preparation, acquisition, financing, investment, or routine internal review.
- **Listing intent**: Is the company listed, preparing to list, considering a listing, or not pursuing a listing?
- **Industry and products**: What does the company actually sell or provide?
- **Business model**: How does it make money? Direct sales, distributors, subscriptions, projects, platforms, OEM/ODM, manufacturing, services, or another model?
- **Approximate scale**: A rough revenue range, asset size, employee count, financing size, or transaction value is enough.

Do not require the user to know a formal industry code or exact financial figures.

## Appropriate intake questions

Ask only when the answer materially changes the top-level scope and cannot reasonably be obtained from available materials:

- If listing is possible: intended board/venue, current preparation or review stage, and reporting period.
- If acquisition/investment: buyer/investor perspective, transaction structure, valuation basis, and key investment thesis.
- If the industry or business model remains unclear: ask what the company principally sells and broadly how it earns revenue.
- If scale remains unclear: ask for only a rough range or permit the user to upload/read available financial materials.
- If the user mentions available files: invite all currently obtained materials in one upload and inspect them before asking follow-ups.

## Matters to defer to the checklist

Do not require the user to answer these before producing the first checklist:

- names, backgrounds, concentration, or transaction details of customers, suppliers, distributors, agents, and other counterparties;
- related-party lists, employee-linked counterparties, fund flows, third-party payments, personal cards, or cash transactions;
- contract samples, revenue-recognition evidence, returns, rebates, collections, inventory details, capacity data, or transaction testing results;
- detailed licences, disputes, IP chains, environmental/safety exceptions, tax exposures, or other facts that normally require investigation;
- known anomalies and management explanations, unless the user volunteers them.

Include these as diligence procedures and information requests under the appropriate workstream. Ask about them later only when reviewing findings or updating scope.

## Beginner-friendly phrasing

Prefer:

- “如果方便，可以先告诉我公司名称；我可以结合公开资料理解公司。”
- “公司主要靠什么方式获得收入？”
- “公司大概属于什么规模？给一个收入区间也可以。”
- “目前是已经确定要上市，还是只是在评估上市可能性？”
- “你也可以把目前已经取得的项目资料一次性上传，我会先阅读再补问。”

Avoid unexplained jargon such as `Fabless`, `QoE`, `IRL`, `cut-off`, or `materiality`. If a term is necessary, explain it in the same sentence.

## Role adjustments

- **Sponsor**: emphasize full-chain verification, working-paper evidence, and regulatory defensibility.
- **Accountant**: emphasize financial assertions, data reconciliation, sampling, confirmations, cut-off, and alternative procedures.
- **Lawyer**: emphasize ownership, authorization, compliance, contracts, qualifications, disputes, and legal consequences.
- **Investor**: emphasize earnings quality, sustainability, downside cases, deal protections, and post-investment monitoring.
- **Management**: emphasize document readiness, control remediation, ownership, timing, and response consistency.

## Stop condition

Start the first-pass checklist when purpose/project type, listing intent, industry/products, business model, and approximate scale are sufficiently clear from user input, uploads, or public information. Obtain role, venue, stage, and reporting period when relevant. Do not delay the checklist to obtain facts that belong to diligence execution. Record unresolved details as information requests, assumptions, or scope-change triggers.
